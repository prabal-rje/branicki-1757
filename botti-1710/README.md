# The Botti letters to Cardinal Gualterio, 1710 to 1713

Abbé C. C. Botti writes to his patron, Cardinal Filippo Antonio Gualterio. DECODE's note names the correspondence; the direction of the letters comes from their content. The year numbers on the pages run from 1710 to 1713.

**State:** from ciphertext: the key came from the cipher text alone. Fourteen word codes have their value from the clerk's decipherment on the page; `key.tsv` marks each one.

**Result:** On the 29 letters that carry a clerk's decipherment, 75% of the shown letters agree with it. The other 22 letters are not checked against a key.

**The cipher:** number codes stand for letters and for words. 46 letters put dots between the codes; 5 letters write them with no dots.

The rows come from my own transcription of the DECODE images. Each English gloss gives the sense of the letter as far as the decoded rows carry it.

## What the letters say

- **1710 (R8659 to R8674).**
  - Botti asks again and again for leave to travel for a few days. He gets no leave, then a short leave.
  - He complains of persecution by the local government, and asks the cardinal for protection.
  - A sword fight and an arrest. An armed clash with the constables ("sbirri"); two officers go to prison; then the affair is settled.
  - Satirical verses against an official; intrigue among church and government officials.
  - Names: Ravenna (R8660, R8661, R8664; also R8717); Rasponi (R8660, R8668, R8670; also R8689, R8691, R8717); Bosetti (R8668 to R8670). The word code for "Ruffo" stands at 19 places in 11 of these letters.
- **The letters without dots, 1710 (R8468 to R8470, R8617, R8618).**
  - A dispute over precedence in the street with the provincial treasurer, Conti (R8469, R8618).
  - A quarrel at Bertinoro that involves don Antonio, nephew of Ruffo; Monsignor Farsetti (R8617).
  - Talk of prison; Farsetti goes to Cesena on the Abbate's order (R8470).
  - Botti is ill with fever; Ruffo orders another injunction ("precetto") against him (R8468).
- **1711 (R8675 to R8703).**
  - A water dispute over broken embankments, and an official inspection of it.
  - Factions among the nobles. Pardons and releases sold for money. A cook sentenced to the galleys is set free.
  - Foreign troops hold a tower. Two missions fail to recover it. Comacchio is named (R8685, R8689, R8691; also R8712).
  - Communal accounts, payments and debts; Botti's own claim stays delayed.
  - An official's long and costly visit; peace rumours; gunfire and grave wounds in a quarrel.
- **1712 to 1713 (R8705 to R8718).**
  - An official's illness is mocked; he spends on puppet shows.
  - The communal dispute is settled. Botti hopes to leave within a few months.
  - Help over a church benefice for a relative. Stronger foreign troops on the border despite peace rumours.
  - A rumoured post at the service of a young royal heir. An archbishop plans a spring visit to Rome.

## What stays open

- **Code 17** (255 tokens): r or q. At its places the clerk writes r 66 times and q 39 times.
- **Code 10** (a): at 208 of 1,830 places the clerk writes u or v. The transcription may read the sign for u as 10. Not checked.
- **Word codes:** about 100 rare codes stay open.
- **The letters without dots** are read with the same key. The division of their digit groups into codes is not sure.
- **Not read yet:** R8468 pages 3 and 7, and R8618 page 2.
- **Five pages with dots are not shown,** because their reading is not sure: R8662 page 3, R8666 page 3, R8674 page 1, R8677 page 1 and R8710 page 4.

## Files

- `records/r<number>.md`: one page for each letter: the English gloss, the rows as read, and the state of each page.
- `key.tsv`: each code, its value, the source of the value, its count, the clerk's agreement at its places, and its doubt.
- `key-page-signs.tsv`: sign labels that some pages without dots use, with their letter on that page.
