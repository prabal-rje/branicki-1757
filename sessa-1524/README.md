# The Duke of Sessa to Charles V, Rome, 1524 to 1525: read with the ledger's key

Luis Fernández de Córdoba, Duke of Sessa, imperial ambassador in Rome, writes to the Emperor Charles V. Thirty records of his letters, April 1524 to June 1525, have cipher passages.

**State:** from key (the cipher ledger's key, with more values that clear texts of the same letters fix). The key is cited here, not copied. The rows mark each value that comes from a clear text.

**Result:** 68.1% of 20,998 cipher units read.

**Read before.**

- Others read some of these letters before: the cipher ledger (https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver) read R9877 and R9878 in part, read R9873, and decoded R9898. Its key rests on the court's own decipherments of several of these letters.
- Clerks of the time deciphered several of the letters, and modern readings of three are in print.
- The Calendar of State Papers, Spain (vols. ii and iii.1), summarises six of them in English.

The rows come from my own transcription of the DECODE images. Each English gloss gives the sense of the letter as far as the decoded rows carry it.

## What the letters say

### April 1524 (Salazar A-31 ff. 79 to 173)

- **14 April** (R9873, its last leaf R9874; R9875 is probably its duplicate; R9876 copies part of it). The French army is breaking up. The Pope will not declare himself in a league, defensive or offensive. Sessa thinks that, under pressure, the Pope would lean to the French out of fear. Lombardy, the coming of the archbishop of Capua, Micer Agostino Foglietta, and the money that the viceroy needs for the army.
- **17 April** (R9881; the same cipher passage in R9879 and R9880). The archbishop of Capua did not leave France content, nor with the talks of peace or truce. Madame the Regent sent a person after him to assure him. The Pope by no means wants to show displeasure with the French. Nothing more is known from England.
- **18 April** (R9877, its duplicate R9878). The Pope's secretary would not show the bishop of Veroli's letters. The Swiss, and the talks of peace and truce.
- **22 April** (R9883, R9884). All the war will fall on the Pope's shoulders. Neither the Pope nor the Florentines have made more provision of money.

### May to July 1524

- **Late April or May** (R9885, R9886, R9887). Little is read. R9887: the Pope, the enemy, Siena, and someone in France who was not content.
- **19 May** (R9889, R9890). The Pope's due fulfilment, friendship, and no more money from the Pope.
- **June** (R9891). The nuncio, the archbishop of Capua, a suspicion, the datary, Alberto Pio da Carpi, the viceroy, money and the war.
- **24 July** (R9893; R9892 is a copy). While the Pope waits for Monsieur de la Roxa, he will not talk of state matters. He shows himself for neither side, so everything is in suspense.
- **29 July** (R9894, R9895). Sessa has spoken to the Pope. The enterprise of France, help, money and the war.

### 1525 (Salazar A-34 ff. 6 to 388)

- **January** (R9896; R9897 of 7 January). The datary and the Pope; Alberto Pio da Carpi; no provision from the viceroy. Madame was content that the second son of the King of France should have an investiture.
- **24 February** (R9898). The Pope and the datary; the datary's attempt to bring the Duke over to the French; money; the Swiss.
- **March to May** (R9899, R9900 of 4 May, R9901 of 18 May). R9900: Lombardy and its need; the viceroy; from England nothing; to enter France; the defence; money; Siena.
- **June** (R9903, R9904, R9905). The Pope is very content that nothing has been done in Lombardy. The viceroy is expected each day. The mother of the King of France sends an ambassador.

## The numbers

- 30 records with cipher rows: 20,998 cipher units; 68.1% of them read.
- 364 more units of mixed rows are clear Spanish words, not cipher.
- R9902 has no cipher rows.
