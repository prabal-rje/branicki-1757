# R4054: Cosimo I de' Medici to Agnolo Niccolini, 11 August 1539

- **DECODE:** record R4054, https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/4054
- **Archive:** Florence, Archivio Niccolini di Camugliano, Fondo antico 214, inserto 1. DECODE's image name: 214_1_146.
- **State:** from ciphertext. Not checked against a key.
- **Rows:** my own transcription of the DECODE images, in `../transcription/`. `../TRANSCRIPTION.md` explains its marks.

## What the cipher passages say

Cosimo writes about the arrest of one Biagio della Campana. The passage also names Giulio, the natural son of Duke Alessandro. These points come from a partial read:

- Biagio della Campana had always kept company with "the sons of Bartolomeo Valori".
- He was taken two years before and examined. Many crimes were found in him, which deserved every punishment.
- A sentence of the Balìa condemned him, and he was sent to the fortress of Pisa. Later he was freed, with many other prisoners.
- "My mother" and one Gian Francesco da Mantova are named, with the words "a suggestione" ("at the suggestion"). The rows then read "di tutti tre insieme … saluare" ("all three together … to save"). They do not say who the three are. A promise of his safety was given "in very great secret".
- Next the rows read "come dal Mantoua … de Mar[z]i era stato ricerco di auel[en]are" ("as through the Mantovano … de' Marzi … had been asked to poi[so]n"). The verb is my restoration: the rows show "auel" and "are", with a gap between. The rows do not show who was asked, or whom the verb concerns. "His most reverend lordship" (a prelate) and Giulio, the natural son of Duke Alessandro, follow.
- This went on without Cosimo's knowledge ("senza saputa mia"). Cosimo's mother is named in the passage ("mia madre").
- Biagio was sought and held when he had left the city, probably to go to Rome. He was then found in prison.
- The last passages name "his most reverend lordship" again, the capture of "this man", and a release.

## A reading with English

The word cuts and the letters in brackets are mine. Words in brackets stand for hidden codes.

- "Biagio del[l]a C[a]mpana … qual sia sempre stata la natura [e] quali[tà] sua … i figlioli di Bartolomeo Valori" — "Biagio della Campana … what his nature and his quality have always been … the sons of Bartolomeo Valori".
- "fu preso dua anni fa e [e]saminato, ritrouo[ssi] in lui tanti errori che meritaua ogni supp[l]itio" — "he was taken two years ago and examined; so many crimes were found in him that he deserved every punishment".
- "… per sent[enza] di Balia era stato [con]dannato secondo che … " — "… by a sentence of the Balìa he had been condemned, as …".
- "… destinato nella rocca di Pisa … liberato molti altri dete[n]uti in carcere per loro demeriti" — "… sent to the fortress of Pisa … many other men held in prison for their crimes were freed".
- "a Firen[z]e doue sempre è stato ben uisto … [co]me nimico [e] delinquente" — "to Florence, where he has always been well seen … as an enemy and an offender".
- "mia madre a suggestione di G[i]an Francesco da Mantoua ser[vitore] ordinario de … de Mar[z]i, di tutti tre insieme … saluare" — "my mother, at the suggestion of Gian Francesco da Mantova, ordinary servant of … de' Marzi, … all three together … to save".
- "promessione della sicurtà sua, datoli in grandissimo secreto, come dal Mantoua … de Mar[z]i era stato ricerco di auel[en]are … sua signoria reuerendissima … Iulio figliolo naturale del duca Alessandro … sen[z]a saputa mia" — "a promise of his safety, given to him in very great secret; as through the Mantovano … de' Marzi … had been asked to poi[so]n … his most reverend lordship … Giulio, natural son of Duke Alessandro … without my knowledge".
- "il det[t]o Biagio fus[s]i cerco [e] ritenut[o]; s'era partito dalla cit[t]à per andarsene a Roma … ese[c]utor[e] … hau[e]sse preuenuto … si trouaua in prigione" — "that the said Biagio be sought and held; he had left the city to go to Rome … the officer … had come before … he was in prison".
- "… sua signoria reuerendissima della cat[t]ura di costui … sia relas[s]ato … obseruantia … sempre … portata" — "… his most reverend lordship, about the capture of this man … that he be released … the respect … always … shown".

## The affair in the histories

The passage fits an affair that the histories tell. This page went online on 19 November 2026. The affair was looked up after that.

- Biagio di Bartolommeo Pesci, "speziale alla Campana", was condemned in 1537 for dealings with the sons of Baccio Valori. He was held in the fortress of Pisa, and later freed.
- In the summer of 1539 he told Cardinal Innocenzo Cybo that he feared a request to poison the boy Giulio. He cited Giovan Francesco da Mantova and "il vescovo di Marzi".
- Cybo told Cosimo on 5 August 1539. Soon after, Cosimo had Biagio arrested. This letter is dated within days of the arrest.
- The record of Biagio's examination, of 27 September 1539, also names "la signora Maria", Cosimo's mother.

The letter does not settle these points:

- The verb "auel[en]are" is a restoration. The rows do not show whom it concerns.
- The rows do not say who "all three" are.
- I do not know if Cosimo's clear draft of this letter survives.
