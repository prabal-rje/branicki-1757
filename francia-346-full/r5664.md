## Summary

Each point ends with its source in brackets. "Decipherment of the time" is the clerk's text between the cipher rows, in a machine transcription. The words are mine. `[?word]` marks a doubtful word.

- The addressee's audiences at the French court show a good intention. If the business is meant in earnest, the parties must now discuss how to reconcile France with the House of Austria. The Imperial side says that it will accept every reasonable condition. (decipherment of the time; "France" and "the House of Austria" rest on the clear copy in R5663)
- The Emperor holds that the French King offended him without cause. He says that he never helped the Duke of Orléans, and that he carried out the peace of Italy. He also gave the Duke of Savoy the investiture of his part of Monferrato. (decipherment of the time; the clear copy names the Duke of Mantua in place of the Duke of Orléans)
- The Imperial court firmly believes that the French King will not leave the Swede. Its ministers say that they hold letters of Cardinal Richelieu. In them, they say, Richelieu assures the Swede that the King will never leave his alliance. So they think that the King's talk of union is "canzoni" (songs, that is, empty words). (decipherment of the time; the clear copy agrees)
- France has begun to put garrisons in the fortresses of the Elector of Trier. Gallas went with ten thousand men to damage Saxony and to draw its troops away from Silesia. A large Swedish force marches toward Bavaria. The forces are balanced, so the arms of France could tip the balance to the side that France joins. (decipherment of the time; "Trier" and "Saxony" rest on the clear copy)
- The writer told the Emperor that the union needs a "libro nuovo" (a new book, that is, a fresh start). The quickest way is to send deputies with full powers to a third place. He had already sent this idea to Mons. [?Bichi]. The Imperial ministers named Innsbruck and Constance. If these do not please, the writer suggests a place of the Catholic Swiss or of the Duke of Savoy. (decipherment of the time)
- In confidence, the writer asks the addressee whether he means to stay in France as ordinary nuncio. He will settle a plan of his own by the answer. (decipherment of the time)

## Notes

- The writer is not named in the letter itself. The endorsement and the header of the clear copy give Grimaldi.
- The letter does not name its addressee. It speaks to a nuncio at the French court. A cover sheet in [R5660](r5660.md) names ciphers "written to Mons. [?Ceva] by other nuncios". So the addressee is probably Ceva.
- "The Swede" in the letters of this file is the King of Sweden, Gustavus Adolphus. He fell at Lützen on 16 November 1632, less than a month after this letter.
