## Summary

Each point ends with its source in brackets. "Decipherment of the time" is the clerk's text between the cipher rows. "Clear text" is the text in clear on f. 415r. Both are machine transcriptions. The words are mine. `[?word]` marks a doubtful word.

- Cristoforo de [?Benavides], the ambassador, has probably reached "cotesta corte" (that court, where the addressee is). He brings the answers that the writer got to his repeated offices. (decipherment of the time)
- These answers are the same as those given to Monsieur [?Bautru]. [?Spain] is ready to make [?peace], if it is good and reasonable, and if [?the recent changes] return "in pristino" (to their former state). (decipherment of the time)
- The addressee may enter the treaty and bring the parties to it. The writer hopes all the more for success because the French side has always shown good disposition, as the addressee and Mons. Bichi have reported. (decipherment of the time)
- [?The Count-Duke] assured the writer that he defers to the Pope's exhortations, and that he inclines to make the business as easy as possible. He said this as talk, not as a declaration. The addressee may use it with care. (decipherment of the time)
- In talk, the writer raised the motion made in Germany to Grimaldi: to send plenipotentiaries to a third place. Mons. [?Liechi or Ziechi], perhaps Bichi, had told him of it. He met no objection. Whether he got a promise does not read clearly. He saw the will and the inclination. (decipherment of the time)
- Madrid hints at complaints about events at [?Trier]. Troops called in by the [?Archbishop] drove out a garrison that had stood there since the time of the Dukes of Burgundy. French troops now hold the place and its citadel. Madrid says that, having been wronged, it must have satisfaction, namely the removal of these changes. Then it shows a great will for a good settlement. (clear text)

## Notes

- The writer is not named on these leaves.
- The writer fears delay: spring is near, and many events could disturb the peace (f. 414v).
- The motion made to Grimaldi in Germany matches [R5664](r5664.md), a letter from Vienna of 20 October 1632. There the writer proposes deputies with full powers in a third place, and Innsbruck and Constance are named to him.
