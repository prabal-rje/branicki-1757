## The leaves

- f. 159v: a docket in clear. It names Cardinal Barberini and sums up the letter.
- f. 160r: the heading gives Rome and the date; the address names Ceva, extraordinary nuncio in France.

## Summary

Each point ends with its source: the decode, the decipherment of the time between the rows, or the clear text. All three are machine transcriptions. The words are mine.

- Rome has Ceva's cipher letters of 30 November and of 3, 13, 20 and 22 December. They came almost together. (clear text)
- Rome praises his prudence and his efforts in the business of the union of the crowns. (decode)
- Ceva must work above all with Cardinal Richelieu, without whom nothing of substance comes. (decode; Richelieu rests on code 581, a conjecture, and on the decipherment of the time)
- He must not let this chance pass. He must take away a pretext to lay every ill on the French. (decode; code 831 is a conjecture)
- The letter names a fear that the death of the Swede and other events may hinder the union. Who fears it rests on code 107, a conjecture. (decode)

## Notes

- Code 334 ("Sueco", a certain ledger value) reads "the Swede". Gustavus Adolphus fell at Lützen on 16 November 1632. The letter is of 15 January 1633.
- Code 581 ("Cardinale Richelieu") is a conjecture of the ledger. The decipherment of the time names Richelieu at the same place.
