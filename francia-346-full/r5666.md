## Summary

Each point ends with its source in brackets. "Clear text" is the letter in clear on ff. 399r to 400r, in a machine transcription. The words are mine. `[?word]` marks a doubtful word.

- Monsieur de Bautru ("Botru") came to Madrid. His mission was arranged with much prudence, amid the noise of the French King's victories. The writer used it for a settlement between the two crowns. (clear text)
- In the talks, the writer found what the addressee had reported: the French King and Cardinal Richelieu are disposed to peace. Bautru had orders to say so to the King of Spain and to the [?Count-Duke]. He found an equal wish for peace at Madrid. (clear text)
- Bautru said that he would hear Spain's complaints and report them. But his instruction did not let him treat. In two or three sessions the writer pressed him to take at least a faculty to treat and confer. He refused. (clear text)
- Bautru was in haste to leave. So that a business of such hope would not die at its birth, the writer sent this courier to the addressee. (clear text)
- The writer asks the addressee to obtain at once a faculty for Bautru, at least to treat and confer on peace. The courier could find Bautru on his way home, at [?Bordeaux] or elsewhere. (clear text)
- Madrid has asked that its own ambassador be sent soon "costà" (there, where the addressee is). He will leave without delay. (clear text)
