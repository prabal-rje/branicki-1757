## Summary

Each point ends with its source in brackets. "Clear text" is the clear copy on f. 395r. "Decipherment of the time" is the clerk's text between the cipher rows. Both are machine transcriptions. The words are mine.

- News came the day before that the Duke of Orléans is fully reconciled with the French King. So the writer renewed, in the Pope's name, the call for the union. He thought that a large idle army in France could move the Austrians to the treaty. (clear text; the decipherment of the time agrees)
- The Imperial side says that it wants the treaty, and that it will not stand on rigour. (clear text; the decipherment of the time agrees)
- But it complains of the French King. He urged the Electors and the other Catholic princes of Germany to bind themselves to him, without a word of the Emperor. Many Catholic princes answered that they would treat nothing without the Emperor. The Imperial side sees in this contempt, and a firm will to harm the Emperor. (clear text; the decipherment of the time agrees)
- After the writer's replies, the ministers said what they want: a sign of the French King's wish for good friendship with the Emperor. A letter of the King, or a spoken message in his name, would be welcome. (clear text; the decipherment of the time agrees)
- The addressee should consider whether to suggest this courtesy at the French court. He must not present the idea as coming from the Emperor or his ministers. The writer has an express order against that. The addressee may present it as his own thought. (clear text; the decipherment of the time agrees)
- If France sends no support to the Emperor's enemies in Germany, the Emperor can expect victories. (clear text; the decipherment of the time agrees)
