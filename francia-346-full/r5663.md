# R5663: a cipher letter of 1632, and a clear copy of a letter from Vienna

A machine transcription of the DECODE images, made in my pipeline. Nobody verified it. Every number here is real.

- **DECODE:** record R5663 ("ASV_i1025_SdS_Francia_346_39"), status "N/A". Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 346.
- **Credits:** DECODE record R5663 (DECRYPT project); Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 346; the key: G. Lasry, B. Megyesi and N. Kopal (2020); the ledger values: the cipher ledger, https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver.
- **Cipher pages:** f. 390r (14 rows).
- **The cipher, as the rows show it:** digits and 15 letter forms read without doubt; 802 sign positions; 25 sign types read without doubt. The digit 6 is 17.1% of the digits read without doubt. Counted apart: 2 doubtful letter forms.
- **It does not read with the known key:** the decode of f. 390r gives 5.31 bits per letter under an Italian letter model. The letters that read give 4.0 to 4.9.
- **The content below comes from the decipherment of the time and from a text in clear.** A clerk of the time deciphered the cipher letter and wrote the text between its rows. This page reports what these texts say, in my words. It does not copy them. No decode of mine exists. My tests found no cut of its rows that fits a letter cipher.

## The leaves

- f. 389v: an endorsement that reads only in part. It gives a year 163[2 or 3] and the word "Cifra" (cipher).
- f. 390r: the cipher letter, with a sparse decipherment of the time between its rows. A docket gives the year 1632. The address at the foot names the nuncio, [?Ceva].
- f. 391r and f. 391v: a letter in clear, without cipher rows. Its header gives Vienna, 20 October, and the name Grimaldi with the title "M.r" (Monsignor). The transcription reads the year as 1630.
- The text of ff. 391r and 391v is that of [R5664](r5664.md), a cipher letter of Vienna, 20 October 1632. So the year 1630 is probably a misreading.

## Summary

Each point ends with its source in brackets. "Decipherment of the time" is the clerk's text between the cipher rows. "Clear text" is a text in clear on the leaves of this record. Both are machine transcriptions. The words are mine. `[?word]` marks a doubtful word.

The clerk's text on f. 390r is spaced letter by letter, and many spans do not read.

- The text names Monsieur de Bautru ("Botru") twice, and Spain. It seems to say that he is sent to Spain. (decipherment of the time)
- It names aid given to [?Monsieur], and an effort to moderate the commissions. (decipherment of the time)
- Someone writes to [?Campeggi], and Bautru must receive notice of something. (decipherment of the time)
- The last lines name something [?insuperable]. They end with the words "they do not want to hear [it] spoken of". (decipherment of the time)
- Ff. 391r and 391v give the Vienna letter of R5664 in clear. See [R5664](r5664.md) for its content. (clear text)
- At one place the clear copy names the Duke of Mantua, where the clerk's text of R5664 names the Duke of Orléans. The clear copy also names the Elector of Trier and Saxony, where R5664 garbles both names. (clear text)
