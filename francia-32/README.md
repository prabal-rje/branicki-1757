# Francia 32: cipher letters of 1590 to 1592, the state of the work

**Another cipher. I did not decode it.** Nothing on this page is read with the known key. The cipher ledger says that it read part of this file with a known key. This page states what each source says, and no more.

## The file

- **DECODE records:** R5612 to R5621 (10 records).
  - Source: DECODE's record pages, as my pipeline saved them on 11 November 2026.
  - The catalogue gives the dates 1590 to 1592, the status "N/A" and the cipher type "Unknown" for each record.
- **DECODE's public page of R5612,** read on 13 November 2026 (https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/5612): status "N/A", cipher type "Unknown", private ciphertext "True". Its key fields are empty.
- **The archive:** Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 32.
- **Scans:** DECODE's image tables list 60 scans. R5619 is catalogued with 5 pages, but its image table is empty.

## What my pipeline read

My pipeline read 10 scans in a pilot: all six of R5612 and four of the five of R5613. Source: the pilot's own summary. It is a machine transcription. Nobody verified it.

- Seven pages are prose or endorsements in clear.
- Three pages hold cipher: R5612 ff. 17r and 17v, and R5613 f. 45v.
- A heading marks one page, R5613 f. 46r, as a decipherment.
- The other 50 scans are not processed. Earlier passes exist for some pages of R5613 to R5616.

## The cipher, as my transcription shows it

Source: the pilot's transcription of R5612 f. 17r.

- Digits, mostly 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8. The digit 6 is rare.
- Many digits carry a mark above them: a bar, a circumflex, two dots or a small cross. The transcription also shows some special signs.
- The cipher of Francia 346 uses a lone 6 as a word space. Here 6 is rare. This is an observation; the catalogue dates (1590 to 1592) and the marks above the digits also set this file apart.

## What the cipher ledger says

Source: the public repository https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver, file `SOLVED_CATALOGUE.md`, read on 13 November 2026.

- **Entry 246** names Filippo Sega, Bishop of Piacenza, writing from Paris to Rome: AAV Francia 32, ff. 17r-v and 71r-v, 26 November 1590 and 16 January 1591. It credits Brice Dudout.
  - It says: "Meister key no. 65 (identified by Lasry for sega1591) applied unchanged".
  - It says: "134 of 136 cipher words read", with the label "read with known key".
- **Entry 324** names Sega's duplicate of early January 1591: Francia 32, ff. 49v to 54v (DECODE R5615).
  - It says: "Meister key no. 65 applied unchanged; 1,091 of 1,319 enciphered words read in their sense (82.7%)", with the label "read with known key".
- The same repository has a folder `targets/sega1590/` with a key table, a transcription, a decryption, a reading and a translation. I did not read those files.

## What is not done

- No decode here. I did not check the ledger's key against my transcription.
- My pipeline has not read R5613 to R5621, except four pilot scans of R5613.
