# DECODE record 5463, second read: the stretches that read

Archivio di Stato di Milano, Carteggio Sforzesco, cartella 1597, DECODE record 5463. One page. Italian, in cipher. No date and no addressee is read.

**This is a partial read from the ciphertext alone, not checked against a key. Twins of this size read .87 of the shown letters right (twin).** A twin is an artificial letter: real Italian, enciphered with a key of the same shape, then read by a simulated sign reader. No key sheet and no clear text checks this read. `check.md` gives what supports it and what does not.

This second read uses our own machine transcription of the DECODE images, not verified. It is not in this repository, because DECODE marks the ciphertext of these letters as private. The earlier read, in `../reading.md`, used the sign transcription of the cipher ledger's maintainer.

The notes of the ledger's maintainer give fragments of this letter: "desideroso", "sperare" and "perche".

## Marks

- The shown form quotes `decryption.txt` exactly. A `·` is a sign with no shown letter, and `…` is a break in the transcription.
- The Italian is our reading of the shown form. The word division is ours. It writes "v" where the decode has "u".
- Letters in [brackets] are letters that we supply.
- […] marks a gap: one or more signs with no shown letter, or a break.
- [?word] marks a doubtful word. Where we change a shown letter, the shown form follows, for example (shown `tenti`).
- In the shown form, `...` leaves out letters that do not read, and ` / ` is a row end.
- "L05" is row L05 of the page in `decryption.txt` and in our transcription.

## What the stretches speak of

The stretches speak of these things:

- friendship and enmity, and the grace of God;
- great obligations to a "magnificent" person, called "father";
- a request for a letter written in a very light cipher, and letters a little more difficult to come;
- practice in writing in cipher: no rule, but long practice and experience;
- a man named Zohane Antonio da Pavia, who sets down [?rules] in writing.

We give these as our reading of the stretches, not as a checked summary of the letter.

## Notes

- The text reads in stretches of sentences, in the chancery Italian of the dispatches. Rows L29 to L32 read less well.
- **The earlier read and the second read.** The earlier read (`../reading.md`) showed "Antonio da Pa·ia", "che spero receuere", "solum per long·· exercitatione et perientia" and "honesto modo per intendere piu". The second read shows the same stretches, on another transcription and from fresh starts.
- **The ledger's fragments.** Our second read shows "desidero·o" (L07), "spero" (L14) and "perche" (L05 and L20).
- The letter model can write a word or a phrase that is common in the dispatches. So a single word proves little. Repeated words and long stretches are stronger.
