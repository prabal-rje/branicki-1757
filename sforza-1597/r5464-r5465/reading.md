# DECODE records 5464 and 5465, second read: the stretches that read

Archivio di Stato di Milano, Carteggio Sforzesco, cartella 1597, DECODE records 5464 and 5465. Record 5464 has two pages. Record 5465 has three pages in DECODE's count. Its second image shows two of them side by side; we transcribe them as two faces of page 2, left and right. Italian, in cipher. No date is read.

**This is a partial read from the ciphertext alone, not checked against a key. Twins of this size read .94 of the shown letters right (twin).** A twin is an artificial letter: real Italian, enciphered with a key of the same shape, then read by a simulated sign reader. No key sheet and no clear text checks this read. `check.md` gives what supports it and what does not.

This second read reads the two records as one text with one key. A declared test could not tell them from one key at this size. `check.md`, section 4, gives the test and its limits.

This second read uses our own machine transcription of the DECODE images, not verified. It is not in this repository, because DECODE marks the ciphertext of these letters as private. The earlier reads, in `../r5464/` and `../r5465/`, used the sign transcription of the cipher ledger's maintainer.

The partial reading of record 5464 by the ledger's maintainer, of 7 November 2026, gave for example "sempre desiderat amici" and "facto stamatina tornare".

## Notes

- **The earlier reads and the second read.** The earlier read of record 5464 (`../r5464/reading.md`) showed, for example, the alliance by marriage, the league for the defence of the states with the Pope, the secret writings, the king and the return to Milan. The second read shows the same stretches, on another transcription and from fresh starts. The earlier read of record 5465 read in patches only and had no reading page.
- Margin and interlinear rows of record 5465 (`(margin)`, `(interlinear)` in `decryption.txt`) read in short pieces only.
- The letter model can write a word or a phrase that is common in the dispatches, such as "celsitudine vostra". So a single word proves little. Repeated words, names and long stretches are stronger.
