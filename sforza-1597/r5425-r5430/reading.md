# DECODE records 5425 to 5430: the stretches that read

Archivio di Stato di Milano, Carteggio Sforzesco, cartella 1597, DECODE records 5425 to 5430. Six letters, which we read together with one key. Italian, in cipher. No sender, addressee or year is read.

**A partial read from the ciphertext alone, not checked against a key; twins of this size read .95 of the shown letters right (twin).** No key sheet and no clear text checks this read. `check.md` gives what supports it and what does not.

The signs are our own machine transcription of the DECODE images, not verified.

**Marks.**

- The shown form quotes the decode of `decryption.txt` exactly. A `/` is a row end. `...` leaves out letters between two quoted parts of one row.
- The word division is ours. It can write "v" where the decode shows "u".
- `[a]` is a letter that we supply. One supplied letter can stand for one or two signs that show no letter (`·`).
- `[?word]` is a doubtful word, or the word that we think the decode means.
- `[…]` is a gap: letters that do not read.
- A row is a row of our transcription: R5425-P1 L08 is record 5425, page 1, row 8.

## What the stretches speak of

The stretches read as the reports of an envoy to a lord. As we read them, they speak of these things. The tables mark our doubts.

- the Swiss ("Sui·eri", read as "Suizeri" or "Suiceri"), the men of Bern, Bern itself and "la confederatione";
- a league, and proposals with their first and second points;
- the investiture of the lordship of Milan, and a great sum of ducats (`·randilima`, read as "grandissima");
- his Majesty and his Holiness, a cardinal, a protonotary and an ambassador;
- the king of France and the wars in France, Genoa, Venice, the Turk, Germany and Hungary;
- the death of a duke;
- secrecy, couriers, replies and letters.

The letter model learned from printed Milanese dispatches of the 1460s and 1470s. Those dispatches name many of these places and people. The notes at the end give this limit.

## Notes

- **"c··e" is "che".** The decode often shows `c··e` where the sense wants "che". Two signs show no letter there. We write c[h]e.
- **"li" where the sense wants "ssi".** The decode shows `ilustrili·a`, `·randilima` and `andilima`, `antiquilisi`, `polibile`, `impreliones` and `proceli`. The sense wants "ilustrissima", "grandissima", "antiquissimi", "possibile", "impressiones" and "processi". So one sign that our key reads as "l" may stand for "ss". We do not change the decode. We mark these words with `[?]`.
- **Names prove little alone.** The letter model learned from printed Milanese dispatches. In that text (not in the cipher), "Franza" stands 742 times, "Borgogna" 402 times, "Savoia" 352 times, "Suiceri" 130 times, "Suizeri" 17 times and "Berna" 46 times. "Prospero" stands 25 times, and "Prospero Adorno" once. So the decode can write such a name where the cipher has another word. Long stretches that make sense are stronger evidence than a name.
- **Some words are not in that text.** "Bernense" and "casolo" do not stand in it, and neither do `antiquilisi`, `polibile`, `impreliones` and `proceli`.
- **The name "Prospero".** The decode shows "prospero", "pro·pero", "pro·pereo" or "pro···ero" on records 5425 to 5430, and "trospero adorno" once (R5427-P L05). It can be a name or the word "prospero" (favourable). We do not settle it.
- **Dates, as we read them.** The last rows of four records hold a day. Record 5426 shows `dieui·e` / `si··oc·aua`, which we read as "vi[g]e/si[ma] oc[t]ava", the 28th. Record 5425 shows `uiesi·aocta`; it can be the same day, but its letters do not fit it fully. Record 5429 shows `uicesimaseconda`, the 22nd, and a month that can be April. Record 5430 shows `·icesimasec··na`, which can be the same day. No year is read.
- **Rows with no stretch** did not read to us. That does not mean that they hold no sense.
