# Morosini, 1587 to 1589: the stretches that read

DECODE, 35 records between R17 and R58 (Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 22 and Francia 18/3). In cipher. The stretches below are Italian. By earlier readings, record R35 is a Spanish memorandum.

Read from the ciphertext alone. I made these readings from my decode, without the key or its decode. The check graded letters, not these readings one by one. `check.md` gives the check.

**Marks.**

- Each row gives the DECODE record, page (p) and line (l).
- The middle column quotes the decode exactly, in the spelling of `decryption.txt`. ` / ` is a line end. `…` leaves out letters that do not read.
- The cipher does not mark word ends, so the words run together. A space stands where the transcriber saw a gap.
- `[165]`, `[166]` and `[167]` are word codes. My read gives them no value.
- The English is my reading of the decode. In the English, a word in square brackets is my addition. It is not in the letters. A `?` marks a doubtful reading.
- The decode often writes "ai" or "i" where Italian has "a". My key reads the digit 2 as i; the published key has a (`check.md`, section 5).

## Names, places and dates

Each item gives my reading and some of its places. Where I give a count, it counts the lines of `decryption.txt` that hold the quoted letters, with spaces left out. A count can include a few lines where the letters belong to another word, for example "eseguise" in R29 p2 l04.

- **The Council of Trent:** "cilio … di trento" and similar. 10 lines hold "trent". For example R18 p1 l02 to l03, R19 p1 l04, R22 p1 l04, R33 p1 l03.
- **Guise:** "di guisa", "mons. de guisa", "signori di guisa". 67 lines hold "guis". For example R17 p2 l03, R18 p6 l03, R20 p5 l01, R33 p1 l06.
- **Navarre:** "il nauaro" (the Navarrese), R20 p4 l05, R21 p5 l22, R45 p2 l06.
- **Épernon:** "mons. de epernone", R21 p5 l12; "epernon", R17 p2 l09, R22 p8 l06.
- **Montmorency:** "montmoransi", R21 p5 l22.
- **Paris:** "in parigi", "de parigi". 17 lines hold "parig". For example R18 p2 l10, R19 p1 l02, R20 p3 l04.
- **The Sorbonne and Chartres:** "la sorbona", R18 p2 l11; "sciartres", R18 p2 l12.
- **Rome:** "a roma", for example R19 p6 l06.
- **Lyon:** "soccorso a lione" (help to Lyon), R43 p1 l02.
- **Orléans:** "governatore de orliens" (governor of Orléans), R34 p5 l21; "d'orliens", R51 p1 l17; R58 p1 l09.
- **Savoy:** "sauoia", R32 p1 l04.
- **The League (?):** "delai legad", R18 p3 l07; "deli legaeli", R33 p6 l24. These can be lega (league) or legato (legate). I am not sure.
- **The King:** "il re", for example R58 p1 l04 and R57 p2 l09; "re … henrico" (King Henri), R57 p2 l16.
- **The nuncio and the legate:** "nuntio", R18 p1 l18, R29 p3 l13. "legato", "legatione", "vicelegato" (legate, legation, vice-legate): 15 lines hold "legat". For example R19 p5 l06, R22 p7 l03.
- **Dates:**
  - "di giugno" (of June), R57 p2 l04 and l07;
  - "di nouembre" (of November), R46 p3 l15;
  - "ilsabatoeil giornodi pascie" (the Saturday and the day of Easter?), R52 p4 l30;
  - "mercordi pasato" (last Wednesday), R58 p3 l05;
  - "lunedirosimo" (next Monday?), R34 p5 l03.

Most items above are in normal spelling, with word breaks. The decode at these places can differ by a letter, for example "i" for "a". Where it differs more, I quote the decode.
