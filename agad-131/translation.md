# AGAD 1/338/0/1/131: English translation

From `reading.md`, with the same holes. The first two paragraphs and the paragraph on Bouwen translate clear Polish. The rest translates the cipher French.

Sir, my especial Benefactor,

Everywhere I carry in my obliged heart a lifelong gratitude to Your Honour. So I do not fail to present my humblest submission to Your Honour.

As to my affair, so as not to trouble Your Honour with long reading, and also because time runs short, I tell it to His Honour the Starost of Brańsk. I ask him to share my letter and its contents with Your Honour.

The Castellan of Brzesc [?Abramowicz] and Count Pociej, Strażnik of Lithuania, are very obliged to His Excellency [?Branicki: 185], Grand General of the Crown.

I told them that His Excellency [?will agree to] declare his protection and obtain the subsidy, the [190], for the coming Tribunal.

They said this. Beyond the subsidy given by France, the Prince Grand General of Lithuania gave a thousand ducats. The Strażnik of Lithuania, the Castellan of Brzesc and his brother [?borrowed] thirty thousand francs from Mr Białłozor, Wojski of [?Pińsk], at the start of the Tribunal. With what they borrowed from the others, the money reached the sum of two hundred thousand francs.

[?So] they beg His Excellency to have them granted, by his protection, for the great subsidy. I ask you very humbly to propose this skilfully, so that I do not fall into disgrace.

The Castellan of Brzesc begs His Excellency, for the memorial of his nephew Mr Ciechanowiecki, Starost of Opsa, lieutenant of His Excellency. He asks him to recommend it to His Excellency the Count de L'Hôpital, ambassador of France. [The end of this sentence is not clear.]

Mr Strutyński, Starost of Szakinów, is known in Lithuania for his disorderly actions, above all in the faction with Mr Ciechanowiecki, brother of the Starost of Mścisław, over his lawsuit. Also his full brother Mr Ciechanowiecki, Starost of Opsa, colonel of His Excellency the Grand General.

Mr Strutyński gathered all sorts of his men, [?...], armed in hand, at the dietine of Brasław, during the sessions, to massacre Mr Ciechanowiecki, Starost of Opsa. And through this family he carried out this [?massacre] on the person of the said Mr Ciechanowiecki, brother of the Starost of Mścisław.

The dead man's body was carried to Wilno and presented in law before the Grand Tribunal of Lithuania, last year. On this his family brought suit against Mr Strutyński, Starost of Szakinów, [?...] for the crime done.

In secret, in the other affair with Mr [?Szkultyn], a gentleman of Samogitia, with whom Mr Strutyński, Starost of Szakinów, was at law over boundaries, he suborned someone to kill him. [?He remained] because of the [?cries].

The said Mr Strutyński did not appear before the court. He withdrew to [?the army] of Russia, to place himself under the protection of His Excellency the Count Apraksin, Field Marshal of the armies of Her Majesty the Russian Empress.

The proceedings were already made against the said offender in the Tribunal of Lithuania, but Count Apraksin has stopped them from going on. He has even opposed, by his own authority, the conviction of this gentleman. This is because of the protection he gives him, on the [?pretext] of [?drawing] him into the service of the Russian army.

They say he did a black deed at [?St Petersburg: 580] about the year 1730, and that the Palatine of Bełz saved him from losing his life.

They also say that Mr Strutyński at [?Danzig: 275] [?tried to betray] King Stanisław.

They say that in the judgment of the Grand Marshal of the Crown there are decrees against his treasurers.

Mr de Bouwen, Secretary to His Highness my Benefactor, excused himself for having no time to write to Your Honour by this post. When I told him that, if he has time, he should write, he went to His Highness, who was in the same room, and said something quietly. So I do not know whether he wrote to Your Honour by this post. He is a [?plain] man,

and he is often under Count Apraksin.

Secret: already the governorships of [?Königsberg] and the other lands of Prussia, and [?...] the palatinate of this province, to the lords of Lithuania, publicly. He has [?promised] the Castellan of Witebsk at Kowno that he will consult [?these] tables, for a memory of ages, [?...] on the [?seals], and [?bring] the council of war.

I give myself to your unchanging favour. I am for all my life Your Honour's, my especial Benefactor's, [?most humble] servant, known by hand.

11 July 1757, from Wilno.
