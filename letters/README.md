The key from `../key.tsv`, found on the Branicki letter 446, reads these with no change. They read as French.

## What they say, in short

**1398, dated 24 July.** A field report on the Russian army under Apraxin near the frontier of [625], likely Prussia. Town deputies come to submit, carts and men are taken without pay, and a rumour says flour and bread were poisoned.

**1399, written in 1757.** An insider's report on Lithuanian faction politics. After a congress at Iwie, the party agrees a fund of 146,000 francs to buy the coming dietines, with the lords' shares listed. The shares add up to the total: 53,000 + 25,000 + 50,000 + 10,000 + 8,000. The writer also sets up a new correspondence at Mitau.

**1400, from Nieśwież, 11 September.** A bloody election assembly at Brest, with massacred gentlemen, brother against brother, and a rival party spreading money across the palatinates.

## Files

Each folder has `reading.md` (French), `translation.md` (English), the transcription and the line-by-line decryption.

## Status

Published 7 November 2026. 1399 and 1400 come from two transcription passes, reconciled on the scan. Page 2 of 1398 comes from one pass and is still rough. `[?word]` marks a doubtful reading.

Numbers are not read as such. In the readings I leave out most of the series 830 to 850, because those numbers fall at clause ends and look like punctuation. The decryption files keep them.

Some numbers are coming clear. The clear text of 1399 has "Mr. le Palatin de Trok" and "Mdme la Princesse Palatine Douairière", and the cipher has "Mr le [575] de Trok" and "Mdme la [822] [576] douairière". So 575 and 576 look like Palatin, and 822 like Princesse.
