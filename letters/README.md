## What they say, in short

**1398, dated 24 July, probably 1757.** A field report on the Russian army under Apraxin near the frontier of [625], likely Prussia. Town deputies come to submit, carts and men are taken without pay, and a rumour says flour and bread were poisoned.

**1399, probably 1756.** An insider's report on Lithuanian faction politics. It plans for the Tribunal of 1757, so it comes before that Tribunal's dietines. After a congress at Iwie, the party agrees a fund of 146,000 francs to buy the coming dietines, with the lords' shares listed. The shares add up to the total: 53,000 + 25,000 + 50,000 + 10,000 + 8,000. The writer also sets up a new correspondence at Mitau.

**1400, from Nieśwież, 11 September 1756.** The year is in the number code: 824 811 823 818 810 reads "mil sept cent cinquante six". A bloody election assembly at Brest, with massacred gentlemen, brother against brother, and a rival party spreading money across the palatinates.

## Outside checks

**The sum in 1399.** The letter gives the total in words: "cent quarante et six mille francs". The shares come later: 53,000 + 25,000 + 50,000 + 10,000 + 8,000. They add up to exactly 146,000.

**The names are in print.** Władysław Konopczyński, *Polska w dobie wojny siedmioletniej* (1909), lists the French embassy's payments in his notes, from the Paris foreign-affairs archives: https://archive.org/details/polskawdobiewoj7v8kono

- Under 1756-7, p. 433: "Matuszewiczowi i Zabielle 1500 duk., p. Vierval 100, Boevenowi 200, wojewodzie bełzkiemu 2000".
- Under 1758, p. 510: "Boevenowi 100".

So the same payment entry holds these people:

- Boeven, who signs 1400.
- Zabiełło, named in 1399.
- Matuszewicz. The signature on 1398 looks like his name, but that page is still rough.
- The palatine of Bełz, Antoni Potocki, who received letter 446.

The same list also pays Branicki, and the cześnik koronny, whom the clear text of 446 names.

**What this does not show.** I searched that book's OCR, and I did not read all of it. I found no copy of these letters there. It's my reading that these letters come from a French-paid network, and it isn't proved. 1399 is unsigned, and "France" for number 350 is a guess.

**The matcher.** My count-matching algorithm reads 1399 and 1400 from the ciphertext alone. Its key agrees with the 446 key on 99.4% and 98.9% of tokens.

## Files

## Status

Published 7 November 2026. 1399 and 1400 come from two transcription passes, reconciled on the scan. Page 2 of 1398 comes from one pass and is still rough. `[?word]` marks a doubtful reading.

Numbers are not read as such. In the readings I leave out most of the series 830 to 850, because those numbers fall at clause ends and look like punctuation. The decryption files keep them.

Some numbers are coming clear. The clear text of 1399 has "Mr. le Palatin de Trok" and "Mdme la Princesse Palatine Douairière", and the cipher has "Mr le [575] de Trok" and "Mdme la [822] [576] douairière". So 575 and 576 look like Palatin, and 822 like Princesse.
