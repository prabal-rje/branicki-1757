# R5521, pages 1, 2 and 3

- **DECODE:** R5521, https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/5521
- **Archive:** Archivio di Stato di Milano, Carteggio Sforzesco, cartella 1598.
- **Pages:** pages 1, 2 and 3
- **State:** from ciphertext. Not checked against a key. Pages 2 and 3 are read with the key of page 1.
- **Language:** Italian
- **Rows:** my own transcription of the DECODE images. The cipher rows themselves are not in this repository.

On page 3 the key shows two faults: the sign for u reads as t, and s and f are sometimes confused.

## Gloss

The writer speaks as "we", a lord, and answers letters about a marriage negotiation ("pratica", "parentato"). A "madonna de Alamagna" is named, and a "monsignore". "His majesty" has given an assurance about the Pope's side. The writer asks that the conclusion not be put off: nothing would please him more than to see it concluded soon, to confirm "with the bond of kinship" the love he bears. He is glad of a testimony that there is no obligation to another marriage, so that no one can hinder the negotiation.

The same lord, writing as "we", goes on with the marriage negotiation of page 1. He holds to his purpose and accepts the conditions as treated: once the betrothal is made, a part of the money in ducats is paid, and the rest later, counted in thousands of ducats. The conclusion was to be made by a bishop, but the royal majesty wants it treated in Rome, "for more dignity". The Pope is unwell: the doctors say his illness comes from a fever, and he may not give audience to ambassadors for some time. A prince has gone to Rome and had an audience. The writer will send the answer of "the most reverend monsignor our brother" by another courier, and names a "monsignor" of Atri (not sure). He wants the royal majesty told of his good will, and the matter concluded.

Page 3: a letter in the name of Ludovico Maria Sforza, marked a duplicate. It speaks of the royal majesty and its wish to treat with speed; of the bishop of Nitra; of the honour of the writer; of Italy; of the Turk; and of provisions to be made with diligence.
