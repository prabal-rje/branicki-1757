# R5521, pages 1, 2 and 3

- **DECODE:** R5521, https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/5521
- **Archive:** Archivio di Stato di Milano, Carteggio Sforzesco, cartella 1598.
- **Pages:** pages 1, 2 and 3
- **State:** from ciphertext. Not checked against a key. Pages 2 and 3 are read with the key of page 1.
- **Language:** Italian
- **Year:** most likely 1492; in any case between 1490 and October 1494. Reason: the pope is ill in the letter, and Innocent VIII died on 25 July 1492. On 2 July 1492 Milan also urged that the bishop of Nitra be sent into Italy. The range: the bishop of Nitra took office in 1490, and Ludovico was duke of Bari until October 1494. Sources: L. Pastor, *The History of the Popes*, on Innocent VIII; A. Berzeviczy, *Aragoniai Beatrix magyar királyné életére vonatkozó okiratok* (Budapest, 1914), no. CLXVII.
- **Rows:** my own transcription of the DECODE images. The cipher rows themselves are not in this repository.

On page 3 the key shows two faults: the sign for u reads as t, and s and f are sometimes confused.

## Gloss

The writer speaks as "we", a lord, and answers letters about a marriage negotiation ("pratica", "parentato"). A "madonna de Alamagna" is named, and a "monsignore". "His majesty" has given an assurance about the Pope's side. The writer asks that the conclusion not be put off: nothing would please him more than to see it concluded soon, to confirm "with the bond of kinship" the love he bears. He is glad of a testimony that there is no obligation to another marriage, so that no one can hinder the negotiation.

The same lord, writing as "we", goes on with the marriage negotiation of page 1. He holds to his purpose and accepts the conditions as treated: once the betrothal is made, a part of the money in ducats is paid, and the rest later, counted in thousands of ducats. The conclusion was to be made by a bishop, but the royal majesty wants it treated in Rome, "for more dignity". The Pope is unwell: the illness causes a burning, perhaps from a stone or a sore of the bladder. He still gives audience to ambassadors, and the doctors think that he can last some time yet. A prince has gone to Rome and had an audience. The writer will send the answer of "the most reverend monsignor our brother" by another courier, and names a "monsignor" whose see does not read clearly. He wants the royal majesty told of his good will, and the matter concluded.

Page 3: a letter in the name of Ludovico Maria Sforza, marked a duplicate. It speaks of the royal majesty and its wish to treat with speed; of the bishop of Nitra; of the honour of the writer; of Italy; of the Turk; and of provisions to be made with diligence.

We opened your letters and understood what they contain. We took a double pleasure. One was the resolution of the said most serene lord not to put off the conclusion of the alliance any longer. All our desire is still for this. Nothing could please us more than to see this negotiation soon concluded. It would confirm, with the bond of kinship, the love that we freely bear [?] for his dignity and eminent virtue.

The other pleasure was to see confirmed what we already believed of His Majesty's sincerity and [religious?] mind. Every report that others spread, that he is bound to another marriage, is groundless. We did not believe it [?]. We are glad to have the testimony that you have given us. When the time comes, it will help to refute what some persons [do] to hinder the negotiation.

The said most serene [lord] holds to his purpose about this our [alliance?] [?]. We too hold to our purpose, well content, and accept the conditions as treated. Once the betrothal is made, we shall give him [?] thousand ducats. The rest, up to [two hundred?] thousand ducats, follows when he takes [her] home. [?] thousand ducats of [?] count in this sum.

The bishop of [Nitra?] was to come to make the conclusion. You write that the royal majesty does not want the matter treated in a place on the border, but in Rome, for more dignity. In view of the illness of the pontiff and of sending the ambassador, we thought it best to send you this courier at speed. We do not wait for anything more from the most reverend monsignor our brother. He could write you no more about this than we do. We have not sent him your letters. If he wants to tell you anything about this, we shall send his answer by another courier.

As to the health of the pontiff: it is true that His Holiness is unwell. The illness is caused by a burning [?]. It is not yet certain whether it comes from a [stone?] or from a sore of the bladder. Whatever the cause, the illness [?]. But the doctors do not think it such that His Holiness cannot last some time yet. He does not stop attending to business and giving audience to ambassadors, as one sees [?]. Above all, the prince of [?] has moved to Rome. He has had, and [awaits?], audience with His Beatitude. At times a fresh [attack?] makes his illness worse than usual, as happens in long illnesses. Even so, he still attends to the dispatch of affairs.

So we think that the coming of monsignor of [Nitra?] should not be [delayed?]. He comes through Germany. We shall place persons at [?] and wherever needed, to wait for him. They will tell him, day by day, what we hear of the state of the pontiff. If the pontiff is no worse than now, he may go on to Rome. There we too shall have messer Ma[?]eo, to conclude the matter in the place that pleases the royal majesty. If the pontiff is worse and does no business when the Nitriense arrives, we shall have him come here to Milan. The matter can be settled and closed here. You write that the [royal majesty?] will be content with this. After this, as the news of the pontiff comes, the Nitriense can go on to Rome.

In Rome there are the authority of the most reverend monsignor our [?] and the name of this state. [?] in the dispatch of his affairs, and so will monsignor [?]. [?] It will be good that the [king?], on his side, sees that good letters are not lacking [?]. Our work will then have greater support. It will have it when [?] understands what we seem ready to do for his gratification. If the affairs of the pontiff [grow worse?], he can take the course most convenient to the affairs of the royal majesty.

So make known to His Majesty, and also to [?], what we have said. Our disposition is good, resolved and free to settle the match on the conditions [treated?]. Tell them also the form of concluding.

#### Page 3

[?] at Rome or here, in the way said above. Show him that he can therefore send the Nitriense with speed, and need not wait on the illness of the pontiff. [?] His Majesty's wish to treat and conclude this matter at Rome [?]. If [the pontiff] grows worse, [the matter will be settled?] here. After Rome, we believe, no other place in Italy is to be put before this one, for dignity and honour.

When you have set out what we write, learn the decision of the royal majesty and inform us with speed. Then we can place persons in time at Padua and wherever needed. They will wait for the Nitriense to pass, and tell him what he must do.

In your [letters?] you touch, to messer Mateo, on the promise of the benefice. You need not doubt this, nor fear that delay will [take away?] the chance to use it. We are well content that the promise take effect with the next benefice of honour that falls [vacant?] [?]. We shall do it very gladly, to reward your virtue as its merit requires. So go on with the diligence and prudence that you have used in this matter, and inform us of the course that will be taken. On our side you will not lack recognition and signs that we esteem your virtue and hold it dear.

Here many reports spread about the preparations of the Turk against this kingdom. So we expected some notice of them in your letters. You wrote nothing particular, and our desire remains as great. We urge you to inform us diligently, on the return of the courier, of these things. Write of the good provisions that we do not doubt this most serene king will make, and of the strength of the Turk's efforts.

Duplicate. Milan, the twentieth of July. [?] Ludovicus Maria Sfortia, duke of Bari.
